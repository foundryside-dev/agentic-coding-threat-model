Traditional Denial of Service exhausts runtime resources such as bandwidth, CPU, memory, or connection pools. The agentic variant extends STRIDE to the development lifecycle: the “service” being denied is the **review process**, a security control, rather than a user-facing system.

This is an analogical extension and the loosest of the six mappings. It concerns depletion of a security control, not an attack on a runtime system.

## Mechanism

This process threat operates at two scales.

At the **individual scale**, agent-generated code arrives at multiples of human review velocity. The queue grows, reviewers shift from semantic review to surface scanning, and the control degrades towards a rubber stamp.

At the **organisational scale**, the same dynamic overwhelms:

- security teams' capacity to evaluate changes;
- the IRAP assessment pipeline's ability to keep pace with system change; and
- the audit function's ability to maintain meaningful coverage.

The degradation compounds from reviewer fatigue into weaker team review and then weaker organisational assurance. A secondary mechanism occurs when automated tools flood reviewers with findings, causing habituated dismissal that hides genuine security issues in the noise.

## Why existing controls miss it

Existing controls assume review capacity scales with generation rate. In practice, the control becomes less effective as the volume it must process rises.

## Risk in government context

Security review can become a compliance checkbox while accreditation continues to rely on assurance that the process no longer provides.
